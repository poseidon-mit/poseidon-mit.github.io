**Slide 2: Structural Issue**

- Problem: The Coordination Gap exists because financial data is fragmented, leaving the user responsible for coordination.
- Market Pain Points: Subscription waste is $133 per month per active user.
- Fraud losses amount to $12.5B annually across the US.
- Overdraft and Non-Sufficient Fund fees cost users $6B annually.
- Conclusion: Fintech resolved visibility issues, but coordination remains manual and isolated from cash flow context.

**Slide 3: Why Now?**

- Environment: Three forces are converging to enable trustworthy personal finance AI.
- Force 1 (Open Banking): APIs are standardizing and data processing rules are clarified.
- Force 2 (AI-Native Expectation): Users expect proactive, personalized financial guidance.
- Force 3 (AI Economics): Inference costs are dropping 10x annually.
- Conclusion: Infrastructure, economics, and user demand converged in 2025, preparing the market for scale.

**Slide 4: Governance by Design Architecture**

- System Structure: Four engines operate as one auditable system: Govern, Protect, Grow, and Execute.
- Govern: Ensures compliance, full auditability, and transparent UI.
- Protect: Deploys personalized ML models for fraud detection, subscription management, and anomaly detection.
- Grow: Provides short and long-term recommendations, cash flow forecasts, and portfolio analysis.
- Execute: Manages human approval-based automated execution, cross-engine linking, and reversible actions.
- Architecture Principle: Deterministic models compute, GenAI explains, AI agents execute, and humans confidently approve.

**Slide 5: Differentiator**

- Commoditized Features: Financial tracking, multi-bank account linking, and rule-based budgeting are standard dashboards.
- Emerging Features: Predictive intelligence utilizing personalized ML models.
- Poseidon Exclusive: Explainable AI with low temperature parameters and approval-first execution featuring human-in-the-loop automation.

**Slide 6: Roadmap**

- Phase 1 (0-3 Months, Foundation): Establish compliance with bank-grade protocols, SOC2, and an AI ethics board.
- Phase 2 (3-12 Months, Frontier): Execute proof of concept, implement LLMOps/MLOps, and launch detection/grow pilots.
- Phase 3 (12-15 Months, Stability): Prove reliability and reach break-even economics with approximately 180K users.
- Phase 4 (15+ Months, Scale): Increase user base to approximately 251K and expand ML model scope.
- Phase 5: Distribution via B2B and White Label channels.

**Slide 8: Business Model**

- Market Opportunity: US retail banking profit is $750B/yr, with $17.5T in low APY balances.
- Unit Economics: At scale, the profit margin is 77%, providing 4.3X value/cost for the customer. Operating break-even is projected for month 12.
- Pricing: Free tier offers dashboard and basic protection. Plus tier ($7.99/mo) assumes a 3.5% conversion rate. Pro tier ($19.99/mo) assumes a 0.8% conversion rate.
- Stress Scenarios: Base scenario expects 3.5% paid conversion and 5% churn, reaching payback in month 19. Pessimistic scenario extends payback to month 31.

**Slide 9: Summary**

- Vision: Establish a trusted financial platform where AI coordination serves human financial wellbeing.
- Governance First: Ensure regulatory expectations are met and every AI decision is auditable.
- Architecture: ML models calculate with precision, GenAI provides explainable text, and workflows orchestrate human-in-the-loop approvals.
- Business Model: Sustainable design targeting 77% gross margin and operating break-even by month 12.

**Slide 12: Risk Register**

- Risk 1 (Model Drift): High impact/likelihood. Mitigated by continuous precision/recall monitoring and threshold-triggered retraining.
- Risk 2 (Security Threats): High impact/likelihood. Mitigated by defense-in-depth, secure SDLC, and incident response playbooks.
- Risk 3 (LLM Hallucination): Medium impact, high likelihood. Mitigated by restricting numeric outputs to deterministic ML models and post-generation validation.
- Risk 4 (Bias and Fairness): High impact, medium likelihood. Mitigated by learning data review and prompt engineering.
- Risk 5 (Regulatory Exposure): High impact, medium likelihood. Mitigated by compliance-by-design, PII sanitization, and control audits.
- Risk 6 (System Resiliency): High impact, medium likelihood. Mitigated by auto-healing architecture and fault tolerance.
